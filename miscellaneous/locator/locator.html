What does each part of this do? (read me first)

Ghost Seeker

Pick whichever Ghost Seek item you're actually holding - check its name in-game. A better one (Grade I is the best) hears farther and tells you your distance more precisely, so if you've got a good one, pick it here. This changes how the app reads your letters, so getting it right matters.

Distance metric - just leave this on the default (Euclidean). You basically never need to touch it.

Add a reading

Every time you hear the Ghost Seeker make its sound, immediately write down where you're standing and which sound you heard. In Minecraft, press F3 to see your X, Y, Z coordinates, or press F3+C to copy a teleport command - you can paste that whole thing straight into the X, Y or Z box here and it'll fill in all three for you automatically.

The letter is just "how far away": A is always the closest sound, and each letter after that means farther away. If you heard nothing at all, pick the last letter (or just type none/nothing/silent) - that means the skeleton is farther than your seeker can hear.

Click Add reading once you've filled it in. Each reading is one clue - the more you add from different spots, the smaller the possible area gets.

Estimate

The search space bar is basically a progress meter. 100% means "could be almost anywhere," and the closer it gets to 0%, the smaller and more certain the remaining area is. Once it's small, that's your cue to go walk over there.

The region shown is a box of coordinates - as long as everything you typed was correct, the skeleton is guaranteed to be somewhere inside that box.

Each reading also gets a confidence percentage - how much your other readings agree with it. If one reading's confidence is low, it's probably a typo, or it was actually describing a different skeleton than the rest.

Conflicts & tracks

Conflicts means two or more of your readings flatly disagree - almost always a typo in the coordinates or the letter for whichever reading you added most recently. Double check that one first.

If the app splits your readings into separate tracks, it thinks there might be two skeletons out there instead of one. That's fine - just pick one track and focus on it, ignore the other set until you're done with the first.

Found

Once you actually find and kill the skeleton, click Found (you can optionally type where). This clears out the readings that led you there so your list stays clean, but it never deletes anything - you can always look back at it under History.

Timer

The seeker's sound repeats on a loop every so often. starttimer just counts down to the next one so you're not standing around guessing when to listen.

Tips: how to actually find it fast

You don't need to know any math or find some perfect geometric spot to stand on. Think of it like a warmer/colder game: every reading draws a big invisible bubble around where you were standing, and the skeleton is somewhere on the edge of (or inside) that bubble depending on the letter. More bubbles from more spots = the overlap between them keeps shrinking, until there's only a small area left where they all agree.

A pattern that works almost every time

  1. Take a reading right where you are.
  2. Walk roughly in a straight line for 50-100 blocks, then take a second reading.
  3. Turn - don't keep going the same direction, turn left or right roughly 90° - walk another 50-100 blocks, and take a third reading.

Three readings from three clearly different directions is usually enough to shrink the search space down to something you can just walk to and check by ear.

Other things that help

  • Watch the search space % after each reading. If it barely moved, that reading probably didn't add anything new - you likely didn't walk far enough, or walked the same direction as before. Go somewhere clearly different next time.
  • Got a conflict message? Don't panic - it's almost always a typo in your most recent reading. Double-check the coordinates and letter.
  • Told there might be two skeletons? Just pick one group of readings and ignore the other until you've found the first one.
  • Once the box in the Estimate panel gets small, walk to it - you'll hear the letters change (getting closer) as you get near.
  • Use starttimer so you know exactly when the next sound is coming instead of standing around unsure whether you missed it.
  • Playing with friends? Everyone's readings stay in their own browser tab - this page doesn't share anything between people automatically, so compare notes out loud (or in chat).
